Stop 0.1 · Start 8 min

Why do a safari website and a Kubrick frame need the same eye?

You build websites for wildlife and safari brands, and you want to direct films. Those look like two careers. Underneath, they use one skill: deciding what a person should see first, what they should feel, and what they should do next. That skill is design, and this course trains the eye before it teaches any style.

Your brief, in your words. Fonts and pairing first. Colour, with Sanzo Wada's combinations. Composition, with the truth about the golden ratio and the rule of thirds. Minimal versus bold. Brand identity. Apple, Jobs and Ive. Caravaggio and Picasso, Kubrick and Spielberg, great architects. And practical use, with real makers, real images and videos. All of it is on the map below.

The research added one thing you did not ask for, and it comes first. Thirteen research passes, including two independent gap audits, found that the outline taught what good design looks like and almost nothing about how designers get there: the brief, how attention really works, value before colour, testing and critique. So Part 1 is the loop before the looks.

Where they split

Time

Film, motion and websites unfold in time; a poster or a painting is seen almost at once. Time adds sequence, reveal and rhythm.

Use

A painting is looked at. An interface is operated. Once someone has a task, usability beats beauty when they conflict.

The body

Architecture and product meet the body: you walk, hold, sit. Scale and material stop being metaphors.

Years

A brand must survive a decade of uses nobody has imagined yet. A film frame only has to work in its scene.

Stop 0.2 · Start 8 min

What is design, if it is not decoration?

In 1972 the Eames Office answered a list of questions about design on film. The first answer is still the best working definition, and it is the test every stop in this course uses.

Philosophy · the definition this course uses
"One could describe Design as a plan for arranging elements to accomplish a particular purpose."

Charles Eames, Design Q and A (1972) · Vitra transcript

Three words do the work. Plan: design is decided before it is made. Arranging: the designer rarely invents the elements, they order them. Purpose: without one there is nothing to judge the work against, and that is when decoration takes over. In the same film Eames answers "What are the boundaries of Design?" with "What are the boundaries of problems?"

Argue with it. Is a Caravaggio design by this definition? What was its purpose, and who set it?

Famous lines you will not find here

Design is full of quotes nobody can source. These four have no primary source that the research could locate, so the guide uses verified lines instead: "Design is thinking made visual" (credited to Saul Bass), "Design is the silent ambassador of your brand" (Paul Rand), "If you can design one thing, you can design everything" (Massimo Vignelli) and "The details are not the details. They make the design" (Charles Eames). The same goes for the statistic that colour increases brand recognition by 80%: no supporting study was found.

The coverage audit

This map was not drawn from memory. Before writing, two independent audits compared it against design school curricula (RISD and Parsons foundation years, the NID Ahmedabad foundation programme, the Bauhaus preliminary course), the tables of contents of the canon (Lidwell, Lupton and Phillips, Dondis, Arnheim, Berger, Norman, Tufte, Block, Albers), practitioner and job standards, and error sources such as accessibility guidelines. Topics that recurred in nine or more sources but were missing became new stops: the brief, perception, value, critique, accessibility, information design and semiotics.

What it proves

The plan covers what those sources treat as core, and the gaps they agreed on are now stops.

What it does not prove

That you can do it. Taste is trained by making and comparing, which is why every stop ends with a task.

The optional shelf

Every stop distils these, so none of them is homework: Lupton and Phillips, Graphic Design: The New Basics; Lidwell, Holden and Butler, Universal Principles of Design; Arnheim, Art and Visual Perception (1954); Dondis, A Primer of Visual Literacy (1973); Berger, Ways of Seeing (1972); Norman, The Design of Everyday Things; Albers, Interaction of Color (1963); Wheeler, Designing Brand Identity (6th edition, 2024).

Design is a plan with a purpose. Decoration is what you get when the purpose goes missing.
