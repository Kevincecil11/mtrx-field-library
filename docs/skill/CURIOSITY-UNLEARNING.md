# CURIOSITY-UNLEARNING (researched Oct 2026, see Field Series No. 00 Part IV)

Kevin's goal: be the person who can learn, unlearn and relearn anything at any age; naturally curious; mentally strong. Curiosity is the ignition, not the engine. Discipline is a designed environment plus recovery, not a fuel tank.

## Curiosity rules for every stop

1. Open with a real anomaly, decision or "why X despite Y", not "Did you know?" (Loewenstein 1994 information gap).
2. Make closure credible: say the route and the time to the answer.
3. Ask for a prediction plus confidence before the reveal (Brod and Breitwieser 2019). Target uncertain-but-not-lost, roughly 40 to 70% confidence (Kang 2009 inverted U; heuristic).
4. Resolve promptly with the causal spine first. Curiosity boosts memory for the answer and nearby info (Kang 2009; Gruber, Gelman and Ranganath 2014).
5. No seductive details before the spine: any fun fact must carry the mechanism or a contrast, else cut it or move it after retrieval (Harp and Mayer 1998; Rey 2012).
6. Never clickbait: honest question, honest answer.
7. Contrast pair: common intuition vs evidence model, plus a near miss.
8. Progress signal: one task attempted before and after.
9. End by converting curiosity to action: retrieval, a novel case, "what would change this answer?", and one new question.

Learner habit: a question ledger (gap, why it matters, confidence, resolved date).

## Unlearning protocol (beliefs, skills, methods)

1. Write the old model and why it feels right.
2. Give confidence and a falsifier.
3. Find a discriminating case (old and new models predict differently).
4. Refute explicitly and minimally (refutation texts g about 0.41, Schroeder and Kucera 2022).
5. Install a replacement causal model with boundaries; corrections alone leave residual influence (Lewandowsky 2012; Ecker 2022).
6. Solve twice with two methods (anti-Einstellung: Luchins 1942; Bilalic, McLeod and Gobet 2008).
7. Retrieve after a delay, test transfer, log updates with probabilities (forecasting training about 8 to 10% accuracy gain, Mellers 2014, Atanasov 2020).

Darwin's golden rule: note contradicting facts at once, because they are forgotten faster.

## Any age (honest)

Adult plasticity is real and task-specific (Draganski 2004; Boyke 2008 older jugglers; Maguire 2000). Synapse Project (Park 2014): demanding new skills 15 h a week improved episodic memory in older adults. Abilities peak at different ages (Hartshorne and Germine 2015: speed about 18 to 19, vocabulary much later). Grammar learning has a sensitive period to about 17 (Hartshorne 2018), and adults still learn. No "rewires your whole brain" claims.

## Discipline and self-help: what to teach

- **Holds up:** implementation intentions d about 0.65 (Gollwitzer and Sheeran 2006); habits need stable cues, median 66 days, range 18 to 254 (Lally 2010), and a missed day does not reset them; WOOP/MCII g about 0.34 (Wang 2021); sleep consolidates memory (Rasch and Born 2013); exercise has a small benefit (Northey 2018 SMD about 0.22); self-determination: autonomy, competence, relatedness.
- **Partly true:** grit (overlaps conscientiousness, Crede 2017); growth mindset (small, d about 0.05 overall, Macnamara and Burgoyne 2023; targeted benefit, Yeager 2019); marshmallow test (much smaller after controls, Watts 2018).
- **Does not hold up:** 21-day habits; ego depletion as a fuel tank (d = 0.04 multilab, Hagger 2016); the 10,000-hours guarantee; affirmations alone; brain-training far transfer.

## Quotes

"Learn, unlearn, relearn" conflates Toffler (Future Shock, 1970) with psychologist Herbert Gerjuoy; it is not Khosla's. "Most adaptable survives" is Megginson (1963), not Darwin. Always verify quotes against primary sources (Quote Investigator, Gutenberg, Darwin Online) before using them in a guide.
