# LEARNING-RULES

Ranked by evidence and leverage. Each rule says what it changes in the guide.

1. **Retrieval practice (strong; Roediger and Karpicke 2006).** Hide every answer until he has tried. End each day with 3 recall prompts.
2. **Spacing (strong; Cepeda et al. 2006).** Reviews on real dates (+1, +3, +7, +21, +60, +180 days). Missed prompts surface in a weak-spots list.
3. **Feedback and relearning (strong).** Every prompt gets a model answer and Got it / Missed it grading, never just a score.
4. **Pretesting (moderate, rising; Pan and Carpenter 2023).** 2 prequestions before the content, one of them a tempting misconception. Wrong guesses are useful.
5. **Interleaving (moderate to strong for discrimination).** Alternate fields and mix earlier cases into review.
6. **Concrete examples (strong).** Each term gets a canonical example and a boundary, with at least one case from his world.
7. **Worked examples (strong).** Show a model answer before asking him to generate. The weekly boss fight is the faded version.
8. **One idea per day (cognitive load).** One central question, 3 to 5 related ideas and one diagram. The rest goes under "go further".
9. **Dual coding without decoration (Mayer).** One labelled diagram per day, plus a plate that carries the idea. Labels go inside the diagram.
10. **Generation and explanation.** A 60-word "explain it to a smart 15-year-old" box, written with the source closed.
11. **Advance organisers.** The map comes before Day 1, and each week opens with its theme.
12. **Calibration.** Self-rate 1 to 5 on Day 1 and again at the end of the week.
13. **Transfer.** One "use it" case per day; the weekly boss fight applies the whole week to a real-world claim.
14. **Narrative hooks (moderate).** Open each day with a genuine puzzle or story and resolve it within the day.
15. **Habit design.** One fixed cue ("after morning chai"), a 12-minute rescue mode, no streak shaming. Never miss twice.

**Myths to avoid:** learning styles, and rereading or highlighting as a primary study method. The highlighter marks what to test.

**Pareto here** means prioritising the concepts that unlock insider conversation. It's a curation heuristic, not a learning law.

## Added Oct 2026 (Field Series No. 00 research)

- Diagnose "read everything, feel empty": the illusion of explanatory depth (Rozenblit and Keil 2002), inert knowledge (Whitehead; Barnett and Ceci 2002), no field map (Chi et al. 1981), no mastery loop. Each guide must fix all four.
- Delayed confidence ratings (Rhodes and Tauber 2011): rate after a delay, then test.
- Compare cases side by side (Gentner 2003: 48% vs 19% transfer). Generation d about .40. Productive failure g about .36 conceptual, about 0 procedural. Teaching g about .35/.56. Mastery d about .52. Deliberate practice explains 4% in education, under 1% in professions.
- Try-it rule: every concept gets a canonical case, a new surface, a non-example, an application and a cue.
- Rhythm: daily 20 (review 4, predict 2, learn 8, recall 4, use and log 2); weekly teach-back, build and top-3 repair; monthly cold test, calibration and weak-spot export; quarterly re-map.
- Myths to never teach: learning styles, 10k hours, rereading or highlighting as study, speed reading, the learning pyramid, Feynman technique as a validated package, "a scheduler guarantees retention", "quiz score proves coverage", "experts can list everything".

## Application audit (5 Oct 2026) and the rule it created

- Audit: the rules were in the skill but applied unevenly. No. 01 had 55 instant checks but no spaced review; No. 02 had warm-ups tied to day numbers, not real dates; No. 05 had checks only; nothing reminded Kevin.
- Rule: every guide ships with the review engine. Finishing a stop schedules its questions on real dates. Reviews use free recall or quiz before reveal, self-grade, confidence rating, interleaved across topics. A miss resets to +1 day and logs a weak spot.
- Reminders: the free Telegram bot in this repo (see docs/ARCHITECTURE.md).
