## Verified corrections (use these, never the popular versions)

- "You have power over your mind, not outside events" is a modern paraphrase. Real line, Meditations 8.47 (Long): "If thou art pained by any external thing, it is not this thing that disturbs thee, but thy own judgement about it."
- "There is no agony like bearing an untold story inside you" is Zora Neale Hurston, Dust Tracks on a Road (1942), not Maya Angelou.
- Angelou's routine, Paris Review 1990: "I rent a hotel room for a few months, leave my home at six, and try to be at work by 6:30." She saved 2.5 to 3 of 9 pages; "I only blew it eighty-six times. Not bad."
- Munger's latticework talk: USC, 14 April 1994. "Invert, always invert" is Carl Jacobi's maxim.
- "Learn, unlearn, relearn": Toffler (Future Shock, 1970) quoting Herbert Gerjuoy, not Khosla.
- "It is not the strongest that survives but the most adaptable": Leon Megginson, 1963, not Darwin.
- Phone "mere presence" brain drain failed replication (2024 meta-analysis d = -0.02). Cold showers cut sick-leave absence, not illness days.
- Ruddy's Godfather pitch is "an ice-blue, terrifying movie about people you love" (Vanity Fair 2009), not "for the people you love".
